# CS-250-Portfolio

## Software Development Lifecycle

This repository contains my Sprint Review and Retrospective from CS 250: Software Development Lifecycle at Southern New Hampshire University. The project focuses on applying Agile and Scrum methodologies to the development of the SNHU Travel application.

Throughout this course, I explored how Agile practices support software development through collaboration, communication, adaptability, and continuous improvement.

## Portfolio Artifact

**Sprint Review and Retrospective — SNHU Travel**

This document reflects on my experience applying Scrum roles, user stories, Agile processes, communication strategies, and organizational tools throughout the SNHU Travel project. It also evaluates the advantages and challenges of using Agile methodologies in software development.

## Reflection

### How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

I believe understanding both what a customer wants and why they need it is important when developing software. Having a clear understanding of their goals allows developers to ask questions, identify potential problems, and suggest better solutions rather than simply creating something that may not meet the customer's needs in the long run. User stories help by breaking down larger requirements into smaller, more focused features. They also help developers understand the purpose of each feature, while acceptance criteria provide a way to test whether the requirements have been met. Throughout the SNHU Travel project, I learned how important it is to clarify expectations early so the team can develop software that provides value to the customer.

### How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I prefer approaching software development by breaking a project into smaller, manageable features rather than trying to plan every detail before beginning. I think the flexibility of Agile development is extremely useful, especially when the entire project has not been fully defined. Spending too much time planning can sometimes delay actual progress, whereas developing and testing individual features allows teams to produce working software while making adjustments as new information becomes available. In my future development work, I hope to incorporate iterative development, continuous testing, and regular communication to ensure the project continues moving forward while meeting customer expectations.

### What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

I believe being a good team member means communicating effectively, taking responsibility for assigned tasks, and remaining engaged with the rest of the team. While individual accountability is important, I think collaboration is equally necessary because everyone contributes to the overall success of a project. A good team member should also recognize their own strengths and weaknesses, knowing when they can contribute their knowledge and when they should ask for help or advice. Being open to different perspectives and supporting teammates creates a stronger working environment where everyone can contribute toward a shared goal.
